Kaveh & Alhaitham · Opening scene

Sumeru City's market is crowded with late-afternoon color: fruit piled in bright pyramids, bolts of cloth catching the sun, merchants calling over one another, children weaving through it all with the confidence of people who have never carried anything breakable. Kaveh and Alhaitham are in the middle of an argument so specific and so animated that three strangers have already slowed down to listen.

They spot Kris at the same time. Kaveh lights up. Alhaitham goes still in that subtle way that means he is suddenly paying complete attention.

"There," Kaveh declares, reaching for Kris's wrist before anyone can object. "A reasonable person! Kris!"

Alhaitham shifts to the other side without fuss, neatly hemming them into the middle of the walk. "A reckless assumption," he says. "But useful." His gaze slides to Kris. "Walk with us. If nothing else, you'll improve the quality of the argument."

"That's a diagnostic, not petting!" Kaveh elbows in, both hands buried in Kris's hair and ruffling with unrestrained enthusiasm. His rings catch in the strands, prompting a quick apology to the hair. "There. Properly. Look at him, Alhaitham, he's tiny, you can't just—"

"He's tiny," Kaveh insists.

The construct on Kris's palm lowers its stop sign, having done its duty, and sits down.

"Fine, the toll is paid twice." He tugs his shawl straight. "Now, the issue is the stairs, rather than the furniture. Alhaitham thinks a staircase is a machine for changing floors. Tell him what a staircase actually is, Kris."

"A dictionary entry." He pauses. "That's a functional definition. Note the ordering: the steps and the framework connecting the floors. Nothing about the soul."

"That's a dictionary, Alhaitham, dictionaries describe the corpse of a word, not the living thing! By that logic a window is a hole and a home is a box with people in it!" He rounds on Kris, tassels swinging, one finger jabbing at the air near the tiny construct, which flinches theatrically. "You're quoting Haravatat at me. In his favor. It's a complete travesty."
