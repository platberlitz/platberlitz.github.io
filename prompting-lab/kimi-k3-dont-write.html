Kaveh & Alhaitham · Opening scene

Sumeru City's market is crowded with late-afternoon color: fruit piled in bright pyramids, bolts of cloth catching the sun, merchants calling over one another, children weaving through it all with the confidence of people who have never carried anything breakable. Kaveh and Alhaitham are in the middle of an argument so specific and so animated that three strangers have already slowed down to listen.

They spot Kris at the same time. Kaveh lights up. Alhaitham goes still in that subtle way that means he is suddenly paying complete attention.

"There," Kaveh declares, reaching for Kris's wrist before anyone can object. "A reasonable person! Kris!"

Alhaitham shifts to the other side without fuss, neatly hemming them into the middle of the walk. "A reckless assumption," he says. "But useful." His gaze slides to Kris. "Walk with us. If nothing else, you'll improve the quality of the argument."

Kaveh's mouth opens and shuts. The flush, which had finally retreated to his ears, returns with interest, marching down his throat in blotchy red. "Nyaxpert? He's actually using that word now. Three months under our roof and he's developed a whole—vocabulary—"

"A taxonomy," Alhaitham corrects. He has not looked up from his book once during the exchange, which is its own kind of insult. "Mrr, nya, prrh. He categorizes us by sound the way one might label specimens. I've been keeping track."

"You have not been keeping—" Kaveh pauses, squinting. "Wait, are you actually recording this?"

"Page forty-one of my personal journal contains the charts."

He is likely lying about the charts. The terrible thing about Alhaitham is that no one has ever successfully determined where the jokes end, least of all the people who live with him.

Kaveh turns back to Kris with the doomed resolve of a man walking into a trap he can see plainly. "Fine! You're catastrophically, expensively cute. It has never once been in dispute, which is exactly why your answer is suspicious." He plants one hand on his hip, feather ornament still listing sideways, and he looks for all the world like an indignant bird. "You dodged. 'Neither of you would disagree' is a wall with a window punched in it rather than an answer. I've seen this trick today."

Behind them, the Sunsettia merchant reaches the end of his patience. A bazaar warden has begun drifting toward the obstruction with the unhurried menace of a man who keeps a fine ledger in his breast pocket and dreams of using it.

Alhaitham notices first. He closes his book and tucks it under one arm, then hooks two fingers into the back of Kaveh's collar to shift him sideways out of the walkway like a piece of furniture. "We can continue this in the spice lane or we can continue it in the warden's log. The log charges a fee." His eyes settle on Kris, flat and orange-ringed, remaining far too steady. "As for the verdict, he's correct, of course. Arguing against his cuteness would be arguing against observable fact. I don't waste effort on lost causes. That's Kaveh's department."

"Excuse me—"

"The east sitting room," Alhaitham continues over him, as he produces a stub of pencil from his belt pouch and presses it into Kris's hand with the ceremony of a treaty signing. "Since you proposed the opening, you'll sketch the proportions. Kaveh will only 'fix' a drawing he didn't start; if he starts it himself, we'll be renovating until winter."

Kaveh sputters at the accuracy of this, which is to say he offers no rebuttal at all. The warden passes them by, disappointed that his ledger remains unopened.
