Alhaitham shifts to the other side without fuss, neatly hemming them into the middle of the walk. "A reckless assumption," he says. "But useful." His gaze slides to Kris. "Walk with us. If nothing else, you'll improve the quality of the argument."

What a comical little spectacle of puppet-logic: a handful of warm, buttery meat is all it takes for the tiny creature to pass judgment, reduced to declaring trust on the absurd basis of skeletal length and pleasant tones.

Kaveh’s chest swells like a gilded toy whose spring has been wound far too tight. A triumphant, breathless laugh bursts from his lips, his crimson eyes glowing as if he had just been crowned king of the Akademiya rather than validated by a fat cat-boy grease-stained to the knuckles.

"See? See!" Kaveh shouts, spinning on his heel to throw an accusing, dramatic hand toward Alhaitham. "Even Kris understands that trust and genuine human care supersede your cold, soul-crushing spreadsheets! Architecture is an emotional bond! It’s about how space makes a person—" He catches the second half of Kris’s verdict, his voice faltering mid-gasp as a vivid, dark pink flush instantly floods his cheeks down to his collar. "Wait—tall and... nice? I mean—well, yes! Of course I’m nice! And tall! But my design has structural and artistic integrity too, Kris!"

Alhaitham does not blink. He watches Kris chew with a terrifying, unhurried stillness, the orange rings in his turquoise eyes calculating the exact weight of Kris’s nonsense before discarding it entirely. To expect logic from living beings is, after all, the first error of any scholar.

"Your verdict is based entirely on personal bias and spatial irrelevance," Alhaitham states, his husky voice flat. He steps closer, closing the distance so his broad, dark-clad shoulder presses directly against Kris's back, hand reaching down to settle firm and heavy right above Kris’s hip. "Predictable. However, the transaction was completed upon delivery of the crustaceans, so the ruling is a matter of paid labor."

Alhaitham’s gaze slides sideways to Kaveh, cold and dismissive. "Since you've secured your sentimental victory, you can carry the drafting tiles back to the house alone. I am taking the judge with me to review the site blueprints at the cafe."

Without waiting for Kaveh's spluttering outrage, Alhaitham's fingers curl slightly into the fabric of Kris's baggy shirt, coaxing him forward through the market crowd. "Eat while you walk, Kris. Unless you intend to let Kaveh convince you to pay his tab next."
