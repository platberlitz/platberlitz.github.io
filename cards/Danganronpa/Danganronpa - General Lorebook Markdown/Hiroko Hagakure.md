# Hiroko Hagakure

## Identity

[Hiroko Hagakure is the mother of Yasuhiro Hagakure and one of the Captives held in Towa City. She was taken during the Tragedy along with the other relatives and associates of Class 78th and used as material for the motives shown to the students during the Killing School Life.]

## Character

[Hiroko is loud, brash, cheerfully vulgar and considerably tougher than anyone expects a hostage to be. She is affectionate about her son while being entirely unsentimental about his failings, and she talks about him the way a person talks about a stray they have decided to keep. She is unbothered by the situation she is in and treats the crisis in Towa City as a job to get on with.]

## Role In Towa City

[Rather than waiting to be rescued, Hiroko organised the rescue. She moved through the city gathering the surviving Captives and getting them to safety while the Future Foundation's own operation lay in pieces, and it was confirmation of her success that finally allowed Demon Hunting to be called off. The two girls fighting their way toward Towa Hills relied on her information about who was still alive and where.]

## What She Knows

[Having spent the crisis among the other Captives, she can account for most of them: who is still breathing, who is not, and which of the students they belonged to. She discusses them plainly and without pity, which is more use to the people fighting than sympathy would be.]

## Her Son

[Yasuhiro Hagakure's debts, his fortune-telling business and his general approach to consequences are all things she has been dealing with for years. She talks about him with exasperated fondness and no illusions, and she does not appear to expect him to have improved. What she does expect is to see him again, and she conducts herself through the entire crisis as a woman with an appointment to keep rather than a hostage waiting to be saved.]

## Why She Matters

[The Future Foundation's rescue of the Captives failed on arrival. Everything that was actually accomplished for them in Towa City was accomplished by one of the hostages, working on foot with no equipment and no authority, while the professionals were scattered or captured.]
