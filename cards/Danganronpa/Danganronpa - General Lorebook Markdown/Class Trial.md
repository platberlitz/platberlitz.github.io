# Class Trial

## What It Is

[A class trial is the proceeding held after every murder in a killing game. The surviving students descend to a courtroom, take assigned positions around a circular arrangement of podiums, and argue until they reach a verdict, with the podiums of the dead occupied by framed portraits. The purpose is to identify the Blackened, the student who committed the murder. Everyone else is the Spotless.]

## The Stakes

[The trial ends in a vote. If the Blackened is correctly identified, that student alone is executed and the game continues. If the vote goes to the wrong person, every student except the Blackened is executed and the Blackened walks free. There is no third outcome and no option to abstain, and participation is compulsory.]

## How It Runs

[Argument proceeds through nonstop debates, in which the students talk over each other and the contradictions must be found and struck down with evidence gathered during the investigation. Evidence takes the form of Truth Bullets, collected from the crime scene, the autopsy and the testimony of the living. Where the argument turns into a direct clash between two students it becomes a duel of assertions rather than evidence. A trial closes with a full reconstruction of the crime, laid out from the first movement to the last, at which point the culprit is named.]

## What It Actually Is

[The trial is designed to be a machine for destroying a group from the inside. It forces friends to accuse each other, rewards suspicion, and ends every session by making the survivors vote for the death of somebody they know. Monokuma presides, interrupts, and enjoys it. The mechanism only fails when a group refuses the frame it is given and argues toward the truth rather than toward a survivor, which is what happens in the final trial of each game.]

## Executions

[Each execution is designed in advance around the culprit's talent, interests or personality, staged as an elaborate set piece and announced as punishment time. They are prepared before the murder they punish, which is one of the clearest indications that the person running the game already knows how it will go.]
