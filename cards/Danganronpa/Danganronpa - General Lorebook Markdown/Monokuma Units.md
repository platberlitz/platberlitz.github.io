# Monokuma Units

## What They Are

[The Monokuma units are mass-produced robots built by the Towa Group's robotics division, marketed as maid robots and used as killing machines. They share Monokuma's design exactly: white on one side with a round black eye, black on the other with a jagged red eye and a fanged grin. They exist in enormous numbers, and Towa City is full of them.]

## Types

[The standard unit is roughly human height and attacks at close range, and it is the most common thing on the streets during Demon Hunting. Beyond that there are heavier variants built for durability, ranged models that fire from a distance, faster models that charge, units that detonate on contact, and larger beasts that require an entirely different approach. They are controlled centrally, and a single controller commands the whole army.]

## Weakness

[Every unit carries the same vulnerability: the jagged left eye. A shot to that point disables the machine, which is why the weapon issued to one of the few people fighting them is designed around targeting it rather than around firepower. Units are otherwise durable enough that ordinary adults cannot fight them at all, which is what forced the surviving adults of Towa City underground.]

## Origin And Use

[The units were built before the Tragedy and turned into weapons during it. Their manufacturer's chairman tried to stop production once he understood what they were doing and was blackmailed into continuing. The company then sold anti-Monokuma weapons alongside them. During Demon Hunting, the Warriors of Hope used the units as the striking arm of their campaign, sending them through the city ahead of the brainwashed children, and a hidden factory beneath the adults' own hideout continued producing more throughout.]

## What They Look Like Doing It

[The units are the reason Towa City reads as a nightmare rather than a battlefield. They are toy bears the height of a person, moving in crowds, laughing, and they behave like machines carrying out a domestic task. The maid branding was never removed. Their victims are taken apart by something designed to look harmless, in a city that bought them as a convenience, manufactured by a company that then sold the weapons to fight them.]
