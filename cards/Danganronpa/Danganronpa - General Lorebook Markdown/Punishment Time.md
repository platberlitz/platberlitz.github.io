# Punishment Time

## What It Is

[Punishment Time is what Monokuma calls an execution. It follows every class trial that reaches a correct verdict, and it is carried out on the student identified as the Blackened. It is not a killing carried out quickly. Each one is an elaborate staged set piece with its own title, its own machinery and its own theme, announced with a slot-machine flourish and watched by everyone.]

## Design

[Every execution is built around its victim: their talent, their interests, or the specific thing about them that made them who they were. An athlete is destroyed by the equipment of their sport. A performer is destroyed on a stage. The design is always a joke at the victim's expense, and it is always precise enough that the survivors recognise the person in it. The machinery is enormous, purpose-built, and finished long before the murder it punishes, which is one of the clearest signs that the person running the game knows exactly how it will go.]

## Other Uses

[Executions are also used to punish rule-breaking outside a trial, and the threat of one is the enforcement behind every regulation in the school handbook. A student who attacks Monokuma, breaks curfew in the wrong way, or interferes with the game's structure can be executed on the spot without a verdict.]

## The Ultimate Punishment

[The final execution of the Killing School Life was carried out by the mastermind on herself. Having lost the vote, she ran through the punishments of every classmate she had killed in sequence, one after another, and called it the Ultimate Punishment. It was the only execution in the game that its victim designed for herself and the only one she enjoyed.]

## In Towa City

[The word carried over into Demon Hunting, where the deaths of the Warriors of Hope in their arenas were staged as executions of the same kind, delivered by the children they commanded or by the destruction of the robots they were sitting inside.]
