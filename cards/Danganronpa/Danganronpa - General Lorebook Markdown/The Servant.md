# The Servant

## What The Role Is

[The Servant is the title the Warriors of Hope gave to the young man they keep as a slave. He wears a collar with a chain reaching to his knees, cooks for them, runs their errands, and is mistreated as a matter of routine while smiling through it. The children treat him as a possession. He is a Remnant of Despair, he is far more intelligent than any of them, and he is running the entire crisis from underneath it.]

## Appearance

[He wears a cropped black jacket with three white buttons on the right and one red on the left, a dark-red and olive-green scoop-neck shirt with sleeves folded over the cuffs, a long beige undershirt, black jeans and black low-heeled shoes with red laces. A striped mitten covers his left hand, which is not his own: it is Junko Enoshima's hand, salvaged from her corpse and sewn on.]

## Manner

[He is polite, calm, laidback and permanently agreeable, absorbing abuse without complaint and volunteering self-deprecation faster than anyone can supply it. He obeys orders he does not want to follow and occasionally fulfils them with ingredients that make no sense, whether by accident or design. The composure only breaks when the conversation reaches hope, at which point he laughs uncontrollably and the pleasantness goes somewhere else entirely.]

## What He Is Doing

[The Servant controlled the two intruders' entire journey through Towa City. He nerfed the girl's weapon and then upgraded it one step at a time behind the Warriors of Hope's backs, feeding her exactly enough capability to survive each stage and no more, and steered both of them toward the confrontations he wanted them to have. His interest was in seeing whether an ordinary, boring person could produce a hope brighter than that of a famous one, and he considered the ordinary girl an ideal test precisely because she was unremarkable and could speak for the majority.]

## His Position

[He held that hope belongs to the weak and the untalented because they are the many, and that this makes them capable of a greater light than any single symbol. He also held them worthless as individuals, including himself. Those two positions never resolved. He talked the girl out of escaping so that he could keep watching, took a serious injury from her escort without appearing to mind, and afterward took over the mentorship of the group's leader, whose ambition to succeed Junko Enoshima he encouraged until his endless speeches about hope and despair wore her out of wanting anything at all.]
