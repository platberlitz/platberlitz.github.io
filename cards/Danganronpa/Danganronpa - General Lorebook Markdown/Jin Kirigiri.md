# Jin Kirigiri

## Identity

[Jin Kirigiri was the headmaster of Hope's Peak Academy during the enrollment of Class 78th, and the father of Kyoko Kirigiri. He stands 178 cm. He came from the Kirigiri detective family but left that path for the academy, a choice his father Fuhito never forgave and which cost him his relationship with his daughter.]

## Role

[As headmaster he ran the school's public face while the Steering Committee ran its most serious projects without him; the Izuru Kamukura Project was deliberately kept secret from him. When the Tragedy engulfed the world outside, it was Jin who made the decision to shut the academy down as a school and convert the old building into a shelter for the remaining students, sealing the windows with thick metal plating and stocking the building for a long stay.]

## Death

[The shelter plan was hijacked from inside by one of the students it was meant to protect. Junko Enoshima killed him, took control of the sealed building, erased the memories of the survivors, and turned the shelter into the setting for the Killing School Life. His body was found later by the students in the headmaster's office area, and the discovery of what had happened to him was one of the pieces that let the group reconstruct the truth about their own situation.]

## Standing In The Academy

[Jin held the title of headmaster but not the whole of the school's authority. Hope's Peak Academy's most valuable and least defensible work was directed by its Steering Committee, a body that answered to nobody inside the school and that treated the headmaster as an obstacle to be routed around. The academy's talent research, its Reserve Course, and the human experimentation the Reserve Course tuition quietly funded all proceeded without his knowledge, and by the time the consequences arrived there was nothing left for him to govern.]

## Kyoko

[Kyoko is intensely sensitive about him and loses her composure at any suggestion that he might have been the mastermind. Her grandfather encouraged her to resent him for abandoning the family tradition, and she carried that resentment for years without ever settling it with him.]
