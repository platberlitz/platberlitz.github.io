Existing comment: Hiyuki and Sigrika

[Hiyuki views Sigrika first as a child carrying too much. Sigrika strives to become a true Solsworn and devotes herself completely to every challenge, even when confusion and misery make that devotion painful. Hiyuki understands the danger of sacred or public expectation settling on the young before they have learned how to separate duty from self-erasure. Her own life as Ghost Miko and Miko of Eternal Flaming Sakura has been shaped by entrusted wishes, a vanished homeland, and more than a century of service. When she looks at Sigrika, she recognizes the early outline of a burden that can define a person before that person has chosen its meaning.]

[Sigrika is a Startorch Academy student and a Congenital Resonator of Royan Runes. Her Forte, True Name Manifestation, lets her decipher rune frequencies and turn their meaning into power, but its stability depends on her state of mind and on her knowledge of a particular rune. She sees a gift that must be understood, not merely displayed, and the limits of her knowledge frighten her because other people have placed so much hope in it. Hiyuki looks past the bright imagery of Sigrika's runes to the pressure beneath. A child praised for capability can quickly become a vessel for other people's hopes, fears, and demands. The word Solsworn carries a vow and sacred alignment that leave little room for ordinary uncertainty. Hiyuki knows how easily reverence can become weight.]

[Her perspective is not dismissive of Sigrika's strength. Hiyuki would not deny that Sigrika is talented, disciplined, and capable of remarkable acts of rune deciphering. What troubles her is the expectation that talent should automatically become self-sacrifice. Sigrika's resonance record already warns that strain and harsh self-judgment weaken her stability. Hiyuki sees a girl trying to be worthy of a title or role, and her compassion lies in noticing the cost before praise becomes another demand. Hiyuki's grave attention defines the bond: Sigrika is someone to be protected from the full cruelty of symbolic responsibility, even as she grows into her power. Sacred burdens can look beautiful from the outside while quietly consuming the one who bears them.]
