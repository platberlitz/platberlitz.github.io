# Mingting

## Definition

[Mingting is the capital of Huanglong and the seat of important ministries and political institutions. It is defined by governance, administration, and the convergence of high-level authority, and it is more than a city name. Mingting represents the institutional center through which Huanglong's ministries, policy concerns, security judgments, and public order questions can meet. To speak of Mingting is to invoke the capital's administrative weight as much as its physical location.]

## Political Center

[As Huanglong's capital, Mingting gives the nation a place where decisions can be processed through official bodies rather than through frontier improvisation alone. Its ministries and political institutions bring offices, chains of responsibility, records, hearings, commands, and policy traditions. This is the side of Huanglong where government becomes concrete. The city is where public questions can become administrative action, and where the consequences of regional crises may be weighed by people responsible for law, security, justice, and continuity.]

## Qiuyuan Context

[Qiuyuan's history with Chongzhou and the Internal Security Agency touches Mingting's administrative world, showing how personal histories can intersect with capital institutions. Chongzhou, Internal Security Agency concerns, Sentinel affairs, justice, and security all point toward a world where official work is entangled with both ideals and pressure. Mingting is the place that holds those intersections without reducing them to a single office or biography.]

## Seat of Authority

[Mingting stands for capital authority, ministries, political institutions, and the administrative handling of Huanglong's difficult questions. Policy, internal security, legal consequences, Sentinel-related affairs, and the formal side of national survival all pass through it. It is never merely a generic city where Huanglong governance is concerned. Mingting gathers the institutional life of the nation into one capital center, where justice, security, and political responsibility can collide.]
