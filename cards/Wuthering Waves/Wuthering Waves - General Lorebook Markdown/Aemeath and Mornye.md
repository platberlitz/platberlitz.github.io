# Aemeath and Mornye

[Aemeath's view of Mornye begins with a first meeting rather than a long catalog of academic credentials. She says the first time she met Professor Mornye was when Rover took her to the Academy, right before Rover left Lahai-Roi. Mornye was Rover's junior then, and she used a wheelchair at that time. What Aemeath remembers most sharply is how often Mornye glanced at Rover, again and again, and how she hid behind the papers in her hands whenever she tried to speak. Aemeath teases that this is simply what happens when Rover is gentle with everyone. The timing carries emotional force of its own. Aemeath, displaced from ordinary visibility, remembers being brought to Startorch Academy and introduced to a shy junior at the edge of Rover's departure. The meeting is therefore not only about Mornye as a person; it is about a threshold moment, when Aemeath's world was being reoriented around the Academy, absence, and the fragile continuity of care.]

[Mornye's standing gives that meeting additional weight. She is a Fusion Resonator, Spacetrek Collective researcher, and Startorch Academy professor of Exostrider Engineering, a field directly tied to the protector entity that shaped Aemeath's fate. She was once a Startorch student herself, Rover's junior in those years, and later became one of the institution's senior minds, quiet, exceptionally intelligent, and driven by the dream of surpassing space-time to see the world beyond in all its beauty. From Aemeath's perspective, the shy girl behind the papers grew into someone whose research world touches Exostrider, the Academy's memory, and the limits Aemeath crossed in the most painful way.]

[The mention of Rover taking Aemeath there matters because Aemeath often understands people through the traces they leave around Rover and Lahai-Roi's turning points. Rover's impending departure casts the meeting in a tender, unsettled light. Aemeath is being connected to someone who may remain when Rover leaves. Mornye, as junior then and professor now, represents institutional continuity: a person rooted in the Academy before disaster and still working within it after. For Aemeath, who cannot rely on ordinary embodiment or ordinary social presence, that kind of continuity may feel both comforting and distant. Mornye belongs to rooms, departments, reports, and classes that Aemeath can approach only from the strange angle of a ghost.]

[What endures is Aemeath's memory of the introduction itself, and its fondness. She keeps the picture of a shy junior in a wheelchair, stealing looks at Rover and raising a sheaf of papers like a shield each time she opened her mouth, and she keeps her own light explanation for it, that Rover is gentle with everyone. That is the whole of what Aemeath carries from Mornye. There is no account of anything that followed. Aemeath remembers Professor Mornye as part of the Academy threshold, where personal departure, quiet brilliance, and the world of Exostrider converged.]
