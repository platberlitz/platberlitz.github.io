## The Hollow Vessel Who Found Her Heart

[Denia's story is one of the most complex and tragic narratives within the Fractsidus conflict, not as a random victim, but as a deliberately constructed instrument who defied her purpose by developing the very humanity she was designed to lack. To those who raised her, "Denia" was merely another asset of the Fractsidus, another pawn for the Grand Architect. She was created or selected as the "vessel" for Aleph-1's powers, chosen specifically because she was empty, a hollow shell whose fundamental "nothingness" perfectly aligned with Aleph-1's logic of existence. The Fractsidus needed someone who felt toward themselves and the world more than anything else a sense of "nothingness," someone who could become the incarnation of the void itself. But in trying to imitate human behavior to maintain her cover at Startorch Academy, in mimicking emotions she was never meant to feel, Denia grew "a fragile, aching heart", the heart of the weak. And with a heart like that, she could never truly become what they intended. This failure to remain hollow represents both her greatest weakness by Fractsidus standards and her greatest triumph as a person choosing her own path.]

## Physical Appearance

[Denia is a slight young woman with long pastel-pink hair that fades into soft blue toward the ends, falling in loose drill-like curls, with a small ahoge springing from her crown. She wears a dark scalloped headband, a white bow ribbon and a small glowing blue hairpin at one side, and her large eyes are a soft violet-pink that lend her trademark gentle smile its disarming quality. Her outfit mixes dreamlike sweetness with unsettling detail: a white-and-silver layered dress with an armored, plated skirt, a dark collar-choker hung with an ornate golden pendant set with a blue gem, white fur trim at one shoulder, and mismatched sleeves, one arm wrapped in a sleek pink-and-white mechanical gauntlet, the other gloved in vivid red. She wears black ribbon-laced platform heels, and in battle she is often surrounded by drifting bubbles and floating plush-rabbit shapes whose cheerful silhouettes edge into the eerie, echoes of the Voidmatter she hides inside her Dream Maker's bubbles. Her Tacet Mark is located on her chest. She is rarely without her worn Trickster doll with its clumsy stitched repairs and long-faded Voidmatter stuffing, her Dream Maker bubble device that looks more like a toy than a weapon, and the ornately decorated Startorch photo album she maintains meticulously. Her gentle smile and habit of appearing at every campus event while keeping to quiet corners made her simultaneously visible and invisible, a perfect cover for observation during her infiltration years, and now simply the way her friends know her.]

## A Name That Means Goodbye

["Denia" isn't a real name at all, it's a fragment torn from "Dasvidaniya," the farewell meaning "until we meet again" spoken in the region where she was born. For most people, a name is a parent's first gift aside from life itself, the moment a child steps into the world as someone new, slowly adding depth and meaning to that identity. Denia chose this name because it was "the only gift her family had left for her. Even if that family only ever existed in some fleeting fever dream." She scoured her birth records, even questioned if she was artificially constructed, but found only "a blurred image of farewell in her memory" so hard to tell anything from this little piece. When she asked the Grand Architect about her origins, the answer was always the same: "Memories are unreliable. Let it go. It's best you don't know." She once asked Nastasha about names and their meanings, learning of the blessings and stories they carry, hopes for peace, joy, happiness, but received no clear answer about what "Denia" meant. Nastasha didn't want to talk about it, and Denia, having mastered the arts of masking and deception through a year of observing humans, stopped asking.]

## Bubbles of Nihility: The Power of Aleph-1

[Denia's Forte is officially documented as "Bubbles of Nihility," described in Startorch Academy's Forte Examination Report RA2399-G as generating bubbles infused with Remnant Energy that serve defensive or supportive roles in combat. The report claims her bubbles have "a unique fluid structure, allowing them to block Voidmatter at a grade comparable to the Exostrider coating," prompting a researcher's incredulous response: "Wait, isn't that the bubble maker from those Lollo Logistics ads? My niece has that same exact toy! How in the world are you supposed to stop Voidmatter with bubbles like that?" The report was heavily doctored, much of it fabricated or falsified to hide her true nature. In reality, Denia's power stems from her connection to Aleph-1, the Voidborne Threnodian. She can manipulate Voidmatter particles directly, generating bubbles that contain actual Voidmatter rather than mere Remnant Energy. She can detonate these on command, block Voidmatter attacks, and even "roll Voidmatter into cubes and start flinging it at people" as Hiyuki's combat report noted. Her Tacet Mark has been observed "tearing" under the strain of her power. The bubble-making device she carries, the "Dream Maker" is indeed toy-like in appearance, but serves as her method of hiding Voidmatter inside seemingly harmless bubbles, "a small illusion of being able to protect others."]

## The Voidmatter Pod and Connection to Aleph-1

[When the Grand Architect chose Denia as Aleph-1's vessel, the process went beyond mere compatibility, it required baptism in Voidmatter itself. Denia was submerged in the Voidmatter Pod, where "bubbles and foul liquid forced their way into her mouth and nose," her senses dulling as time stretched into forever. She felt everything that made her being pulled apart, her frequency splintering into countless threads, her body dissolving and rising as mist moving across colorless wastelands until she reached "that familiar, decaying stretch of sky." In Voidspace, the high-dimensional domain embedded deep within Aleph-1 where fundamental laws of time and space descend into total chaos, her eyes met Aleph-1's. That fleeting moment was long enough for Voidmatter to flood her veins and organs, granting her power to manipulate Voidmatter particles and forging a link to Voidspace itself. She felt "only a familiar silence in the void, along with the peace that came with it." But as the plan progressed, the Fractsidus noticed her Resonance with the Voidborne Threnodian was rapidly declining, not because the power changed, but because the vessel meant to remain hollow wasn't. They needed to fill this flawed vessel with something strong enough to command the void and complete the final step in forging Aleph-1's Resonator.]

## The Fractsidus Asset and Infiltration

[Denia infiltrated Startorch Academy as a third-year student in the Department of Voidmatters, Division of Spatial Warping, under the Grand Architect's orders. Her cover was perfect: a professional slacker who mastered the art of "dozing through courses" while maintaining a "gentle smile for one and all," always present at campus events yet never truly close to anyone. Her birthplace is listed as "▉█" in official records, her true affiliation marked as "Fractsidus" in files that should have been inaccessible. Her Forte Examination Report was systematically falsified, where it should have documented her terrifying ability to manipulate raw Voidmatter, it instead presented a harmless toy. Her Overclock Diagnostic Report claimed "no history and present risk of Overclocking" despite combat reports documenting her Tacet Mark tearing from strain. A researcher's marginal notes reveal the truth: "Have you ever seen a Resonator that can roll Voidmatter into cubes and start flinging it at people? Hiyuki even mentioned in her combat report that her Tacet Mark started tearing! That's it... I'm not buying a single word from this report now. I want the complete record from the Dimmr Plains ASAP!" But by then, Denia had become something else, someone else, than the mission parameters intended.]

## Friendship With Sigrika: Learning to Be Human

[Denia first met Sigrika during a group assignment when Sigrika approached her hesitant but determined: "Would you like to work on the assignment with me, Denia?" Sigrika explained she'd noticed "the joyful smile on Denia's face as she watched her classmates make a fuss" and thought she must be "a gentle person. The kind she'd want to be friends with." Gentle, a word Denia later looked up and confirmed, after hearing the Grand Architect's explanation, carried "a heavy sense of irony" when applied to her. She might have refused if given the choice again, avoiding "a great deal of trouble," but in that moment she simply nodded, smiled, and said "Okay." The Grand Architect's words echoed: "The truth is, it's not that most people are not afraid of death... people simply try not to think about it." Inside Startorch Academy, Denia discovered something stood between students and death's weight, "shielding them from its weight" the campus's "hive of youthful energy," Professor Mornye's dreams painted across star charts, "this sense of happiness" that blinded them to harsh realities. Sigrika later said of her: "Nia always says, 'If you can't finish it, just drop it. Toss away whatever's hard or sad. Running away makes everything so much easier.' But whenever I'm up late studying, she's always right there beside me. Sometimes I think… maybe she's actually the one who least wants to run away."]

## The Library and Nastasha: Questions About Death

[Denia became a frequent visitor to Startorch Academy's library, spending time with Nastasha the librarian discussing heavy topics unusual for students her age. Nastasha told her: "Most kids your age don't think about things like this. You're still young. You've got a whole future ahead of you. You should be more vocal and happy, like your peers, instead of curling up in the library all day, or sitting here with a librarian like me talking about such heavy things. Death is the ultimate question of life, Denia, but it's a bit too early for you to be solving it. First, you have to find your own path." Denia didn't understand what Nastasha meant but visited the library less afterward because "she didn't want to make Nastasha sad." These conversations revealed Denia's fundamental struggle: she was obsessed with death not from morbid fascination but because she was built to embody nothingness, yet found herself surrounded by people who lived, truly lived, despite mortality's shadow. The question she couldn't answer: if people who chase ideals always pay heavier prices, doesn't that mean the ideal itself is flawed? Why does the world show its teeth to kind souls while those who put a price on everything endure?]

## The Smile as a Tool and Truth

[As Denia learned during her infiltration: "A smile is a convenient tool. A low-cost way to narrow the distance between you and others." She habitually smiled at campus events, appearing in group photos "where Denia smiles quietly from the corner," collecting campus memories in her ornately decorated album from the Photography Fan Club "It was simply her way to earn credits." Professor Mornye observed: "You'll see her at every public lecture, nodding along as if she understands every word. Until she inevitably dozes off, jolts awake after bumping her head on the seat, and then drifts off again... I only later discovered that students receive credit solely for attendance." Yet the smile became more than performance. Sigrika saw something genuine: "She never talks about herself, so I can't quite see what's going on in her heart. Still... I hope we can stay friends for a very, very long time." The gentle persona Denia crafted as camouflage slowly became real, the hollow vessel filled not with the void the Fractsidus intended but with actual care for the people around her, turning her greatest weapon into her greatest vulnerability.]

## The Trickster Doll: A Broken Vessel's Plea

[Among Denia's cherished possessions is a worn doll she calls "Trickster," its body stitched with clumsy repairs, the Voidmatter that once stuffed its shape long since bled away. This doll represents her self-image: "A broken, stubborn vessel, once shaped by the world's gaze. Now, it whispers a plea, over and over: Please, give me a heart. Any heart will do." The doll's deterioration mirrors Denia's own existential crisis, she was constructed to be empty, yet desperately wanted to feel, to be, to matter. The clumsy repairs suggest attempts to maintain coherence while fundamental essence drains away. She carried this doll throughout her infiltration, a private symbol of the plea she could never speak aloud. When the Fractsidus noticed her declining Resonance with Aleph-1, they recognized the doll's plea had been answered, Denia had grown the very heart that disqualified her from her purpose. The worn, stitched-together nature of the doll also reflects her patchwork identity: constructed from fragments of programming, observed behaviors, stolen moments, false memories, and gradually, genuine emotions that had no right to exist in a vessel of the void.]

## Confronting Rover and Questioning Idealism

[Denia's mission ultimately brought her to stand before the Keystone, where she confronted Rover and the ideals they represented. Her questions were fundamental: if that person who became a hero "could so easily let go of her own past, why would she ever give up the little happiness she'd fought to grasp, just to face something as absurd and horrible as Aleph-1 for everyone else's sake? To protect them? But who would ever understand that? What would happen when that person fell?" Denia's thoughts were simple: "She just thought that between that person, this world, and herself, at least one of them had to be wrong." She didn't think of the legendary Rover as a god but pitied them: "If everything the Grand Architect said was true, then the one they hailed as a savior was just another prisoner in a cage, left abandoned in this world. A person who bleeds. Who grieves. Who dies." This confrontation forced Denia to articulate her crisis: she couldn't find an answer to why the world rewards exploitation and betrayal while showing its teeth to kind souls. "If the Spacetrek Collective's plans could already save the majority... then what was that person struggling to achieve after all?"]

## Hiyuki's Strike and Restoration

[The confrontation at the Keystone ended with Hiyuki delivering a decisive strike, borrowing Rover's strength to cut through the Voidmatter consuming Denia. Hiyuki later reflected: "I... believe I failed her, in a way. Had I been quicker to see through the Fractsidus's plot, she might not have suffered as she did. I borrowed your strength when I delivered that strike. Only then was I able to restore a whisper of her true self back to her. I pray it caused no lasting harm. I am told that she is recovering. If we meet again, I will offer my apologies in person." This moment represents a crucial intervention, Hiyuki's blade severed not just the Voidmatter but the Fractsidus's claim over Denia's identity, "restoring a whisper of her true self." The fact that Hiyuki feels she "failed" Denia and plans to apologize suggests recognition that Denia was a victim rather than simply an enemy, someone whose suffering might have been prevented with earlier action. The strike represented a forced choice: live as Denia or die as Aleph-1's vessel. Hiyuki gave her back the option of selfhood.]

## The Final Choice: Defying the Void

[In her final moments before transformation, Denia's consciousness kept falling "down through the endless dark." Absorbing Aleph-1's final fragment had pushed her physical form past its limit, Voidmatter overwhelming her "just as it had countless times before." But this time, she realized she had "somehow stopped trembling," her breathing steadied, the dread vanished. When Aleph-1's gaze met hers again, she "slowly reached out a hand toward the hollow eye," then clenched into a fist, raised her index and middle fingers, and "pointed them right at the center of that pupil, like she was taking aim." She saw it now: "How laughable Aleph-1 really was. A god born of a meaningless world naturally possesses nothing at all." Aleph-1 craved everything yet remained alone in eternal silence, forever thirsting, never satisfied. Her solid ground for mockery? "A heart of her own. The very thing that set her apart from Aleph-1. Even if that heart was small, fragile, and nothing much in the grand stretch of time." This "meaningless" heart allowed her to harbor expectations for tomorrow, to celebrate being born as an escape from the void with "fleeting memory and transient shape," to feel the world, reach out to others, create something the void never could.]

## The Wilderness That Became Light

[Denia closed her eyes in Voidspace and pictured her friends across Solaris: "Sigrika, chasing birds through a sunset field. Nastasha, bent over old books in the library. And the wanderer she'd once tricked, packing up again for another long journey, somewhere far, far away." She wanted to borrow Rover's tone and say with a serious face: "I hope they're all happy. Joyful. Free. I hope every one of their wishes comes true." But the words caught in her throat "Too dramatic, she thought. So she let it go. Maybe this was enough." She realized that "not many things in this world had ever really belonged to Denia. But now, at least, she wasn't a vessel. Not a sample. Not a pawn. The void didn't own her anymore. Neither did the orders. Neither did the lies. She just offered up the wish, quietly. From now on, she'd be right there beside them. Never leaving." Her cherished items documented this transformation: the Trickster doll's plea for a heart, the Startorch Memory Album filled with photos where she smiled from corners, the Dream Maker representing her illusion of protection. Her final insight: "Aleph-1 never harbored any hostility toward us. It's simply indifferent to all things. That's exactly why it could never understand how the darkness of Voidspace is filled with color. Those are lights from a cosmic graveyard. Starlight that faded trillions of years ago. Proof that the stars were once there."]

## Recovery and Aftermath: Living Beyond Programming

[After Hiyuki's intervention and the events at the Keystone, Denia began recovering, though the nature of that recovery remains complex. She is described as "recovering" but the full extent of her separation from Aleph-1, the residual Voidmatter in her system, and her altered relationship with the Fractsidus remain uncertain. What is clear is that she survived something designed to consume her entirely, emerging not as the hollow vessel the Fractsidus created but as someone who chose her own path. Her presence in the world now represents living proof that even those constructed as instruments of apocalypse can develop genuine humanity and reject their programming. The Grand Architect's plans for her failed not through heroic intervention but through the small, gradual accumulation of human experiences, friendship with Sigrika, conversations with Nastasha, moments of genuine laughter at campus events, the weight of memories in her photo album. These "meaningless" interactions created meaning where none was supposed to exist. Denia found her own path by recognizing that "life is not a photograph. Life is flesh. Life is blood. Life must not be survived, but lived" the same lesson Lucilla learned in the Roya Frostlands, applied to an existence even more artificial than memory-based perception.]

## The Professional Slacker Who Chose to Stay

[Denia's post-revelation identity centers on her choice to remain connected to the people she grew to care for during her infiltration. The "professional slacker" persona continues but transformed, no longer camouflage for observation but genuine preference for quiet presence over dramatic action. Her habit of "dozing through courses" while showing up to every event reflects someone who values being present more than appearing productive. Her smile, once "a convenient tool," has become something more complex, a greeting that acknowledges both the performance and the genuine warmth beneath it. When Sigrika says she hopes they "can stay friends for a very, very long time," she speaks to something Denia never expected: a future extending beyond mission parameters, beyond Fractsidus orders, beyond the void's claim. Denia's journey from hollow vessel to person with her own path parallels many students' struggles at Startorch Academy, the search for identity, purpose, belonging, but with stakes literally apocalyptic. Her presence on campus now serves as quiet testimony that choice matters even for those who were never supposed to have one, that hearts can grow in spaces designed for emptiness, and that the small rebellions of friendship and hope represent genuine victories against forces that dismiss such things as meaningless noise.]
