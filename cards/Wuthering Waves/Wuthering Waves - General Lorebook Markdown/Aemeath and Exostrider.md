# Aemeath and Exostrider

[Aemeath's view of Exostrider is the most solemn of her relationship entries because it is inseparable from the transformation that cost her body. She says that for years after becoming a digital ghost, she would journey to the Frostlands just to see it. Exostrider stood there, silent and motionless in the snow, and she would stand beside it, quietly watching. Her recollection reaches back toward a time before the dawn of Lahai-Roi, when Exostrider still carried the Reactor Drive that was its primary energy source. Exostrider removed that Drive and set it burning as a sun to nourish Lahai-Roi, then entered dormancy without it. It guided the Roya Tribe underground and provided the components that became the Exoswarm, and through them the ecology of Lahai-Roi was restored. Why it chose to give up its own heart, Aemeath does not know, and she still asks it. Exostrider is not just a machine, not just a protector, and not just the project that changed her fate. It is a presence she returns to like a grave, a companion, and a monument.]

[Exostrider is a protector far older than the institutions that now study it, and Startorch Academy trains the Synchronists who can reach it. Aemeath is its Synchronist. That bond was never a simple cockpit assignment. When a Threnodian moved to break the seal, she resonated with Exostrider from the cockpit to stop it, and her body was torn apart in the process. The cost was catastrophic, yet her later visits do not read as hatred. She does not describe Exostrider as a culprit. She describes it standing in snow while she stands beside it. The quiet is mutual, even if only one of them is speaking in memory. Aemeath's grief attaches itself to proximity rather than accusation.]

[From Aemeath's perspective, Exostrider becomes a fixed point after the rest of her existence has become unstable. She is a digital ghost, unseen by most people, moving through Lahai-Roi without the ordinary body that once made campus life, Frostlands travel, and human conversation simple. Exostrider, by contrast, remains visible, heavy, still, and anchored, dormant since it surrendered its Reactor Drive. Its motionlessness may be painful, but it also gives her somewhere to return. In the snow, she can measure her own persistence against the silent protector who gave up its own power source for a world it would not walk in. Watching it is a way of remembering who she was and still is: a Synchronist, a student, a person who reached for a protector and continued afterward as signal.]

[Exostrider's importance is filtered through Aemeath's long afterlife of return. What matters is not every function of the protector, but what Exostrider means to the one who lost embodiment through their bond. Aemeath's quiet visits transform the Frostlands scene into a ritual. She cannot undo what happened in the cockpit, reclaim the simple life before it, or force the silent figure to explain why it tore out its own Drive. She can still go there. She can still stand beside it. In that repeated act, the bond survives as loyalty, mourning, and unresolved companionship under snow.]
