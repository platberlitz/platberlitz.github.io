Latest Version: 15.1

This is a universal preset that should work with most LLMs. 15.1 keeps the rewritten main prompt at around 900 tokens depending on tokeniser and spends them better: continuity tracking, an environment that actually affects people, real agency for you, and a dialogue section with some teeth in it. Grounded Prose Rules remains an optional 600-700 token layer, now with firmer wording for models that treat every ban like a suggestion. Tested on just about most LLMs I can get my hands on: Opus, Gemini, GPT, GLM, Kimi K3 and K2.x, Qwen, StepFlash, Mimo, Gemma 4, Muse Spark 1.2, Tencent Hy3, MiniMax M3, and smaller models like Rocinante 12B, Cydonia 24B, WizardLM 8x22B, and Bielik 11B.

IMPORTANT! If you're using a smaller model, keep Post-History Instructions turned off. It will probably mess it up anyway, it has a lot of *avoid* and *banned* statements that will just make the LLM do it more.

The idea behind the prompt was that I like to make the LLM go the direction I want it to, and I wanted it to write for me, since I'm lazy. In the end, it turned into a preset that adheres to a writing style I personally enjoy. Instead of simply being functional, I wanted the prose, the world, and the characters to sound lively, while removing as much slop as I possibly can.

Prose Voice is its own narration layer, with Disabled as the default. 15.0 credits the authors behind each voice and properly adds Gossipy Voyeurism, based specifically on Bret Easton Ellis' American Psycho.

NPC naming rules and banned names now have their own toggle instead of hiding inside Formatting. There is also a new User Is Not A Character experiment for staying entirely in the director's seat, best paired with a persona named Narrator or Director.

Prefills & Reasoning now houses Reasoning Encouragement and the Experimental Anti-Overthinking Prefill. The latter has been getting Kimi K3 down to roughly a minute of thinking in my testing instead of letting it redraft until the heat death of the universe.

Turn off reasoning if, like me, you're not a fan of waiting, but it's not necessary. This preset works regardless, even in terms of anti-slop.

I designed the preset to be as easily customisable as possible, so hopefully that works out for your personal use case! All trackers are optional and token-efficient. My intent for them is actually not for the LLM, but for me, since I like pretty things. It doesn't really hurt the LLM if you don't activate all of them (I mean, you can, I did it, but it's probably not optimal), but you don't need them, either.

  • SillyTavern version: the full preset with all toggles: trackers, randomisers, and everything else
  • SillyBunny version: a trimmed version with only the Main, Primary Toggles, and Prefills; trackers and randomisers are now built into SillyBunny itself

Core Prompting

Director Main Prompt
SillyTavern preset SillyBunny preset

The main brain of the whole thing. I rewrote it from scratch for 15.0 and got it down to about 900 tokens. It still does the director framing, characters acting on their own, scenes that move without being shoved, and no parroting your last message back at you - just without the baggage it used to carry. 15.1 adds continuity tracking for who did and said what, an environment that actually affects people, stakes that stay proportional to the scene, and a dialogue section that grew from one line to five.

Prose Voice
SillyTavern preset SillyBunny preset

Narration styles, Disabled by default. 15.0 credits the authors each one is built on and finally makes Gossipy Voyeurism work properly. The rest are Conspiratorial Absurdity, Bureaucratic Irony, Cosmic Playbook, Beige Undercurrents, Cruel Realism, Solemn Witness, Grand Satirical Stage, and Randomised if you can't decide.

Grounded Prose Rules
SillyTavern preset SillyBunny preset

The optional 600-700 token anti-slop layer that sits in post-history. 15.0 says "never" instead of asking nicely, because too many models were reading the bans as a wishlist. Smaller models still do better with it off - telling them not to do something is usually how you get them to do it.

Formatting
SillyTavern preset SillyBunny preset

The output rules. Present tense, third person, dialogue in quotes, no headings, no asterisk narration. The name randomiser used to live in here and kept breaking prompt caching, so it moved out into its own toggle. 15.1 drops {{dialoguecolors}} in here because it's easier to keep track of as a macro. It needs the Dialogue Colours extension, so just delete that line if you don't use it.

Length Toggles
SillyTavern preset SillyBunny preset

Four nudges for response length: Flexible, Short, Medium, Long. Flexible is on by default and now explicitly tells the model to keep ordinary scenes short, which has the happy side effect of cutting down overthinking. Only pick one.

Simplified Director Main Prompt
SillyTavern preset SillyBunny preset

A ~600 token version of the main prompt for smaller models, or if you're watching your token spend. Keeps the world logic, the direct style, and the anti-echoing, loses the finer nuance. Fair trade at that size.

Genre
SillyTavern preset SillyBunny preset

A light tone anchor. The default keeps things immersive and grounded without tipping into melodrama, but this is the easiest place to drop in your own genre bias if you want a different flavour.

Optional User Instructions
SillyTavern preset SillyBunny preset

A slot for whatever you want. It gets injected as a Main Prompt macro when enabled, so the model takes it more seriously than a note buried further down. I use it for relationship rules and recurring scene notes.

Primary Toggles

Write for User
SillyTavern preset SillyBunny preset

The model writes everyone, you included, but it still has to move things forward and stop somewhere that actually needs your input instead of looping the same beat back at you.

Don't Write for User
SillyTavern preset SillyBunny preset

The default. 15.0 spells it out with "never": write everyone except <user> and the director, add real pressure or information, then stop when the next move is yours. 15.1 goes further - the moment a character turns to you and waits for a reaction, the scene ends there instead of helpfully answering on your behalf.

Name Randomiser + Banned Names
SillyTavern preset SillyBunny preset

Keeps NPC names varied and blocks the same handful every model reaches for. If I never see another Elara or Kael it'll be too soon. It sits outside Formatting now so you can turn it on without wrecking prompt caching. Randomiser macro by Geechan, banned list by Deigo.

User Is Not A Character
SillyTavern preset SillyBunny preset

Experimental. Keeps you out of the cast entirely so you're purely directing. Works best with a persona actually named Narrator or Director, otherwise the model gets confused about who you're meant to be.

HTML Toggle

Lets the model render in-world objects as real HTML. Letters, maps, posters, menus, screens, receipts, dossiers, warning signs, anything where the layout is carrying part of the information. One of my favourites, honestly.

CYOA Choices

Direction Menu

Four narrative pivots at the end of each message. Not small actions - whole directions the next beat could go in: escalation, rupture, complication, or a shift in tone.

Summary

NSFW Mode

Stronger erotic framing without immediately turning the whole RP into porn. Charged atmosphere, explicit language once you're actually there, and characters who commit instead of fading out at the door.

Gooner Mode

Friction Mode

The gentler anti-positivity-bias option. Characters agree slower, are harder to impress, and stop trying to therapise you, while still behaving consistently with their motives. If you have ever wanted a character to just say no, this is the one.

Makes the world mean. Trust becomes conditional, everyone protects their own interests first, and good outcomes have to be earned through leverage, persistence, or actually losing something. Don't turn this on if you want a nice time.

GPT Assistant Prefill

Experimental Anti-Overthinking Prefill

For models that second-guess themselves into oblivion. In my Kimi K3 testing it keeps thinking down to roughly 300-1000 tokens, about a minute, rather than letting it redraft over and over. 15.1 adds the HTML guidelines to the checklist, since models kept reasoning about the prose and then forgetting the markup existed.

Trackers

Scene Tracker
SillyTavern preset SillyBunny via Agents

A header for location, time, and atmosphere, but only when the scene genuinely changes. It shouldn't be turning up on every ordinary reply, and if it is, something has gone wrong. 15.1 also stops it handing you an empty block - if the tags are there, the description has to be in them.

Time Tracker
SillyTavern preset SillyBunny via Agents

Tracks time that actually moves: scene jumps, sleeping, hours passing, deadlines creeping up. Keeps the pacing legible instead of letting three days blur into one conversation. 15.1 asks for an actual hour, 3:47 PM rather than 'late afternoon', unless the setting genuinely makes the time unknowable.

Relationship Tracker

Affection and trust meters for recurring NPCs, updated when something real shifts. I didn't want to gamify every exchange, just mark the moments that changed something.

Pending Events Tracker

Quests, promises, threats, deadlines, loose threads. It only updates when something is added, resolved, or falls through, and 15.0 is much clearer about ignoring the low-stakes junk that used to clutter it.

Achievements Tracker

One-off tags for scenes worth remembering. Most scenes get nothing at all, and it caps at one. It should feel earned, not like a mobile game handing you a trophy for opening the app.

Reputation Tracker

15.1

  • No new prompts, nothing moved, nothing removed. This one is just 15.0 with the rough edges filed down after actually using it for a few weeks.
  • Main Prompt
    • Added continuity tracking for who did what and who said what, and the environment now has to actually affect people - weather, lighting, temperature, how much space there is.
    • Body proportions have to stay sensible. Height, width, weight, how far limbs and people are from each other. I got tired of tails reaching to your ankle. It's still probably gonna happen though.
    • You get agency written in properly now. The plot shouldn't move without you, and stakes stay inside the scene instead of going apocalyptic in three replies. Major offender here is GPT 5.6 Sol.
    • Characters have to earn their own self-analysis, with psychological barriers depending on who they're speaking to. No more handing you the full trauma backstory on first meeting.
    • The dialogue section went from one line to five. No parroting your words back unless the character is literally a parrot, dialogue carries its own information without a paragraph afterwards explaining what it meant, and stammering, trailing off and misspeaking are all fair game without a follow-up sentence spelling out the subtext. GLM tends to overexplain what this and that means - hopefully this mitigates that.
    • Dialogue and action get mixed together without collapsing into short choppy beats, and the rhythm varies line by line.
    • Softened the omniscience rule. Characters can be all-knowing if the story actually supports it, which matters if you're playing gods or anything with a seer in it.
    • 'Easy similes' is now 'unimportant similes', plus an explicit line against overwriting.
  • Prompt-level Tweaks
    • Don’t Write for User: when a character directly addresses you and waits on a reaction, the scene ends right there. Should cut down on it answering for you.
    • Formatting: added {{dialoguecolors}}. You need the Dialogue Colors extension for it, otherwise it just sits there doing nothing. I added it there because it's easier to track with the Macro option. Just remove it if you don't use it.
    • Flexible length: now tells it to check the previous turns to work out how long the scene should be, instead of guessing fresh every time.
    • Experimental Anti-Overthinking Prefill: added HTML guidelines to the checklist and made the permissive wording blunter, because some models were still hedging halfway through.
    • Impersonation prompt: spells out that it's only your actions, thoughts and dialogue. It kept narrating for everyone else in the room.
  • Trackers and RPG bits (SillyTavern build only, the SillyBunny one doesn't ship these)
    • Scene Tracker: the description between the tags can't be left empty any more. I kept getting hollow [SCENE] blocks with nothing in them.
    • Status and Conditions Tracker: only truthful status effects, things that actually happened in the story. It was inventing conditions nobody had.
    • Time Tracker: the hour has to be specific now, 3:47 PM rather than 'late afternoon', unless the setting genuinely makes the time unknowable.
    • Persona-Based Stat Generator: utility skills are generated from your setting instead of the fixed Lockpicking/Analysis/Repair/Medicine/Survival list. That list was a leftover from when this thing was Fallout-flavoured, and I forgot to fix it because I'm a mouth open closing goldfish.
  • Settings and fixing typos
    • Exported at 256k context, reasoning effort on high, media inlining off. These are just wherever my sliders happened to be, change them to suit your model and your patience.
    • Typo fixes: 'charactets' in the Main Prompt, and Franz Kafka's name in the Bureaucratic Irony voice. That one had been wrong since I wrote it, sorry Franz.

    • Level Up Companion: Levels up your level number, adds traits/perks based on how the story progresses and what you choose.

SillyBunny

v1.7.0 · based on SillyTavern 1.18

A SillyTavern fork that me and two others have been building. Same data, same extensions, except the shell around it actually makes sense now.

It exists because I got tired of hunting through nested drawers to change one setting. Everything sits in a top bar split into Workspace, Customize, Home, and Characters, the bottom bar is a permanent input field, and the settings are layered instead of piled on top of each other. There's a home page with tutorials and recommended extensions, and a search bar that actually searches everything at once - presets, lore, extensions, personas, settings - rather than making you guess which menu it's hiding in.

It also ships with things already set up, because I think handing someone an empty frontend and wishing them luck is a bit rude. You get the Bunny Guide and Assistant Nahida cards, presets from me, Geechan, and TheLonelyDevil, and a curated extension list you can install from inside the app.

It's still beta and we're three people, so things do break. If it's broken in SillyBunny, tell us on our tracker. If it's also broken in normal SillyTavern, that one's upstream's problem.

  • Drop-in compatible with your SillyTavern characters, chats, presets, and extensions. Point it at the same folder and it just works.
  • Two chat modes: the usual Roleplay view, and Conversation Mode, which turns a character into a messaging app thread.
  • The SB version of Pura's Director Preset is trimmed on purpose - the main prompt stack stays in the preset, trackers and randomisers moved into built-in agents.
  • Default port is 4444. Bun by default because it starts faster, Node.js launchers bundled for the platforms where Bun isn't worth the trouble.

What's Different

The stuff I actually wanted out of a frontend

Graphical Shell
The top bar splits everything into Workspace, Customize, Home, and Characters, with shortcut slots you can fill with whatever you actually use. The bottom bar is a permanent input field, so persona switching, chat switching, search, and guided generations are one tap away instead of buried three drawers deep. That was the whole point, really.
Chat Modes
Roleplay is the normal view, nothing surprising there. Conversation Mode is the fun one - it turns the whole UI into a messaging app, with scheduling, statuses, follow-up messages, selfies, and memory handling. Much more casual way to talk to a card. Heavily based on TheLonelyDevil's Discord Pals, go look at that one too.
Mobile-Aware Layout
Phones and tablets get their own navigation and spacing instead of the desktop page crushed down to fit. 1.7.0 fixed a lot of the keyboard and touch behaviour that used to drive me up a wall.
More Personal Customization
Six shell themes - Windows Aero, Clean Minimal, macOS Minimal, Cozy Warm, Hypr Glow, Slate Flat - plus 54 colour themes, saveable accent profiles, and three message styles. There's also a wand button in the Custom CSS menu that asks your model to write the CSS, for when you know what you want but not how to type it.
Bun-First Runtime
Bun is the default because it starts faster and is kinder to your battery. Termux, macOS, and ARM get Node.js automatically since Bun is a headache on those, and you can force either one with the runtime-specific launchers. On something small, SILLYBUNNY_BUN_SMOL=1 runs it in low-memory mode.
Same Data, Same Extensions
Characters, chats, presets, themes, and extensions all carry over. It's a different shell for the same roleplay folder, not a lock-in fork. You can go back whenever you want.

Agent Stack

Small models doing the boring work so the good one can write

In-Chat Agents
An agent is just a stored prompt with its own conditions, stage, connection profile, and output handling. Toggle them, group them, import and export them, or pin the ones you use constantly to Quick Toggles. Each one can run on a different model than your main chat, which is the part I use most - a cheap model doing tracker work while the expensive one writes prose.
The Pipeline
Four stages. Pre-generation agents inject rules, conditions, or trackers before the main model reads anything. Then the main model writes. Sidecar agents run alongside it for commentary that isn't part of the reply. Post-generation agents do a second pass over the finished text, which is where prose polishing and course correction happen.
Companion Agents
Companions make their own model calls and store the results instead of touching your reply - that's how the tracker panels get filled in. They can depend on each other, hand context between themselves, and run all at once or one at a time. Parallel if you're impatient like me, sequential if your provider starts complaining about rate limits.
Why the SB Preset Is Leaner
The SB preset keeps the shared core - main prompt, prose rules, primary toggles, prefills. Everything else, the trackers and formatting helpers and directives and randomisers, moved into agents you can switch on and off. Same stack, just not all crammed into one file.
Bundled Pura Ecosystem
SillyBunny ships my templates and groups already set up, browsable in the app and sorted into Tracker, Randomizer, Content, Companion, and Custom. Pick the pieces you want instead of importing one enormous preset and then turning two thirds of it off.

Trackers, randomisers, formatting helpers, content toggles, preset groups, and passes that rewrite or append after a reply. 1.7.0 added Level Up Companion, User Stats, and CYOA Choices with Skill Checks.

The Ethereality Express

The Ethereality Express

Latest Version: 1.0

Download 1.0 Regex SetSillyTavern Regex SetMarinara Engine

The magical realism sibling of Pura's Director Preset. The impossible is mundane, nobody explains it, strangeness follows emotional logic, and the prose stays flat and direct. The total opposite of the grounded realism in the director preset. Main prompt is roughly 1300 tokens.

This is specifically made for a world that literally lives - so expect strange things to happen, such as objects talking to you, being followed by fruits, and so on. The style is written to be as strong as possible to push the model into hard magical realism. Turn on Anchor mode if you'd like a break from all the weird stuff. I tried to make this preset as easy to edit as possible; hopefully you feel that you can edit it as you like.

By default, these are on: Drift, Omen, Hour, Voice, Becoming, and the Census. Everything else in Preset Options is off until you switch it on.

Veil Modes set how much strangeness a scene is allowed. Toggle at most one; with none on, the main prompt's own default level applies.

Trackers are all optional. Each one makes the model print a small labelled block of text, which a regex then renders as an animated card. Toggle whatever you like, but running all fifteen at once means fifteen sets of formatting rules competing for the model's attention, and output quality drops well before that. Poor model…

While I tried to make it compatible with NSFW and intimate scenes, your mileage may vary, as always. It's possible it won't mesh with the average person's idea of intimacy; after all it's supposed to be combined with magical realism.

In my experience it seems to ensure the prose is less sloppy, however it is more metaphorical. If you find descriptive prose to be automatically purple, this isn't for you.

Tested on Kimi K3, GLM 5.2 and 5.3, Deepseek V4 Pro and Flash, Gemini 3.7 Flash, Gemma 4 31B, Gemma 4 26B A4B, Gemma 4 12B (ran locally), Opus 4.6 and 5, and Fable 5. Still need to do further testing for other usual ones, but so far so good.

Inspirations: Authors in Narration Voices and Disco Elysium.

Preset Options

What each toggle actually does

One file, no SillyTavern and SillyBunny split this time. 1.0 ships ready to run: the Liminal Main Prompt, Genre, Formatting, Flexible length and Don't Write for User are on, along with six trackers - Drift, Omen, The Clock Is Lying, The Committee, The Becoming and The Census. Everything below that is off until you switch it on. Grab the regex set from the Preset tab too, or the trackers arrive as raw brackets.

Toggle whatever you like, but running all fifteen trackers at once means fifteen sets of formatting rules competing for the model's attention, and output quality drops well before that.

Core Prompting

Liminal Main Prompt

The whole preset, around 1300 tokens. You're the overseer, impossible things are ordinary, and characters treat the strange the way you'd treat a leaky tap.

Ethereal Prose Rules

The post-history anti-slop layer, which bans the word 'ethereal' in a preset called The Ethereality Express. Leave it off on smaller models.

Genre

Two sentences telling the model what magical realism actually is, mostly so it stops staging strangeness as a big reveal.

Formatting

Present tense, third person, dialogue in quotes, no asterisk narration. Delete the {{dialoguecolors}} line at the bottom if you don't use the Dialogue Colours extension.

Length

Short is 3-5 paragraphs, Medium is 8-13, Flexible reads the scene. Pick one; I run Flexible.

Write for User / Don't Write for User

Pick one. Don't Write for User says never rather than asking politely, and ends the scene when a character asks you something directly.

Optional User Instructions

An empty slot for whatever you want to add on top. I left a joke in there about characters turning into tiny goblins, so clear it out first.

NSFW

The same flat, physical register as the rest of the preset, with the strange turning up in it and leaving something behind. If that isn't your idea of intimacy, leave it off.

Veil Modes (one at most)

Veil Thin

Two or three strange things a scene: rooms rearranging by mood, doors leading somewhere adjacent. The loudest of the five.

Anchor

One small strange detail per scene at most, with the rest spent on chores, money and people being irritated. I use it to cool down after something big.

Elegy

Strangeness through memory and absence, like a coat that keeps its owner's posture. Grief stays practical.

Feral Wonder

Folk-tale rules, so the impossible bargains, keeps score and sends a bill. My favourite of the five, honestly.

Farce

The ridiculous played completely straight, all committees and paperwork for miracles. The stranger the event, the drier the sentence.

Narration Voice

Generational Murmur

Márquez. Long, warm sentences that treat decades and dinner with the same patience.

Quiet Static

Murakami. Cool and plain and faintly detached, anchored in cooking and laundry, with the strange arriving sideways.

Lucid Geometry

Calvino. Playful precision, rooms and relationships handled like elegant machines with rules.

Velvet Teeth

Carter. Rich, sensuous and a little dangerous, where desire and hazard share the same detail.

Randomised

Rolls one of the four each reply. Chaotic, and I enjoy it more than I expected to.

Chance

Unscheduled Phenomena

A silent d20 every reply decides whether reality amends itself partway through the scene, and a natural 20 brings a second amendment later in the same reply. The threshold sits in the prompt as a plain 14, so raise it for a calmer world.

Trackers

Drift

A needle measuring how far the scene has moved from ordinary, MUNDANE up through to SUBLIME. It only files when the level genuinely changes.

Motif

Counts recurring symbols and what they've picked up: objects, animals, phrases, weather. It logs continuity rather than meaning, or the model starts writing an essay about itself.

Omen

Signs the world plants about what's coming, DISTANT through to ARRIVED or PASSED OVER. Readings are allowed to be wrong.

Thin Places

Locations and hours worn through at the elbow: the pantry, the sixth stair, 3:03 AM. Once a thin place has rules, those rules stick.

Absurd Trackers

Entanglement

Relationships as physical objects, like a red thread at the wrist or a debt of three teaspoons of sleep. One form per pair.

The Clock Is Lying

Official time against actual time, both correct at once. It says 3:47 PM, the truth is 'two griefs past noon'.

Improbable Effects

Inventory, except the possessions have moods and demands. Anything borrowed from the strange accrues interest.

Afflictions & Blessings

Impossible conditions people catch, inherit or earn, like casting two shadows, one of them punctual. Cures always cost something specific.

The Ledger

Debts owed to and by the strange: three true compliments, one afternoon of childhood, the good scissors. It always collects, always slightly askew of the terms.

What the Town Knows

Rumours kept like livestock, hatching and feeding and mutating. They drift towards the satisfying version rather than the accurate one.

Department of Small Miracles

An unseen bureaucracy issues deadpan certificates for notable impossibilities, form numbers and office hours included. One per scene at most.

Meanwhile, Impossibly

Three bullets of off-screen strangeness every few scenes, tagged with how close it's getting. It accumulates until it walks into the scene.

The Thought Cabinet

Straight out of Disco Elysium. Ideas move into a head as tenants, then either settle in permanently or get evicted and leave a dent behind.

The Committee

Also Disco Elysium. Four to six named inner faculties interrupt mid-prose with a pass or a fail, and a failure stays confidently wrong until events correct it. Mostly fluff; it isn't tied to any skills or numbers.

The Becoming

The story stitches people an epithet out of whatever they keep doing. The Apology Landlord. The Tuesday Widow.

The Census

Registry-style profile sheets for new named characters, sorted into RESIDENT, LODGER and PASSERBY. Nobody is ever seen collecting it.

Doors & Dice

All off by default. Two regexes in the set strip old Doors and Winds menus out of what gets sent to the model, so they don't pile up in your context; the copies already in your chat log stay visible where they are.

Doors

A choice menu of 3 to 7 options at the end of every response, some treating the impossible as an errand and some entirely mundane. One is always slightly ill-advised.

Doors with Fate Checks

The same menu, except every option carries a competency and target number from your Almanac and is resolved by one hidden d100 whose arithmetic never reaches the page. Pick this or plain Doors, never both.

The Four Winds

Four plot pivots at the end of every reply, two grounded and two where the impossible arrives at the worst moment. Sits fine alongside Doors.

Almanac Generator

Rolls you a stat sheet compiled by a local clerk who finds none of it remarkable. Turn it off once it has printed or it keeps regenerating.

The Turning

Level-ups, kept rare. A season only turns after something that mattered, so ordinary wins accumulate in silence and get nothing.
