Pura's Director Preset

Latest Version: 16.0

This is a universal preset that should work with most LLMs. 16.0 rewrites the main prompt again, now around 1300 tokens depending on tokeniser: it stops forcing a plot jump every reply, ends on a proper handoff to you, tells your OOC notes apart from scene material, and gives characters vocabulary that comes from their own lives. There's also a new Diegetic Stats Mode, and the Relationship Tracker is a dating sim card now. Grounded Prose Rules remains an optional 600-700 token layer, now with firmer wording for models that treat every ban like a suggestion. Tested on just about most LLMs I can get my hands on: Opus, Gemini, GPT, GLM, Kimi K3 and K2.x, Qwen, StepFlash, Mimo, Gemma 4, Muse Spark 1.2, Tencent Hy3, MiniMax M3, and smaller models like Rocinante 12B, Cydonia 24B, WizardLM 8x22B, and Bielik 11B.

IMPORTANT! If you're using a smaller model, keep Post-History Instructions turned off. It will probably mess it up anyway, it has a lot of *avoid* and *banned* statements that will just make the LLM do it more.

The idea behind the prompt was that I like to make the LLM go the direction I want it to, and I wanted it to write for me, since I'm lazy. In the end, it turned into a preset that adheres to a writing style I personally enjoy. Instead of simply being functional, I wanted the prose, the world, and the characters to sound lively, while removing as much slop as I possibly can.

  • SillyTavern version: the full preset with all toggles: trackers, randomisers, and everything else
  • Neconyan version: the plain preset with only the Main, Primary Toggles, and Prefills; Neconyan ships it by default and has the trackers and randomisers built in as agents
More notes

Prose Voice is its own narration layer, with Disabled as the default. 15.0 credits the authors behind each voice and properly adds Gossipy Voyeurism, based specifically on Bret Easton Ellis' American Psycho.

NPC naming rules and banned names now have their own toggle instead of hiding inside Formatting. There is also a new User Is Not A Character experiment for staying entirely in the director's seat, best paired with a persona named Narrator or Director.

Prefills & Reasoning now houses Reasoning Encouragement and the Experimental Anti-Overthinking Prefill. The latter has been getting Kimi K3 down to roughly a minute of thinking in my testing instead of letting it redraft until the heat death of the universe.

Turn off reasoning if, like me, you're not a fan of waiting, but it's not necessary. This preset works regardless, even in terms of anti-slop.

I designed the preset to be as easily customisable as possible, so hopefully that works out for your personal use case! All trackers are optional and token-efficient. My intent for them is actually not for the LLM, but for me, since I like pretty things. It doesn't really hurt the LLM if you don't activate all of them (I mean, you can, I did it, but it's probably not optimal), but you don't need them, either.

Core Prompting

Director Main Prompt
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

The main brain of the whole thing. I rewrote it from scratch for 15.0 and got it down to about 900 tokens. It still does the director framing, characters acting on their own, scenes that move without being shoved, and no parroting your last message back at you - just without the baggage it used to carry. 15.1 adds continuity tracking for who did and said what, an environment that actually affects people, stakes that stay proportional to the scene, and a dialogue section that grew from one line to five. 16.0 rewrites it again at around 1300 tokens: a reply can be a single quiet beat now, Scene End became Turn End and hands the scene back to you properly, and OOC notes stop being treated as canon.

Prose Voice
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

Narration styles, Disabled by default. 15.0 credits the authors each one is built on and finally makes Gossipy Voyeurism work properly. The rest are Conspiratorial Absurdity, Bureaucratic Irony, Cosmic Playbook, Beige Undercurrents, Cruel Realism, Solemn Witness, Grand Satirical Stage, and Randomised if you can't decide.

Grounded Prose Rules
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

The optional 600-700 token anti-slop layer that sits in post-history. 15.0 says "never" instead of asking nicely, because too many models were reading the bans as a wishlist. 16.0 adds accounting metaphors ('ledger', 'files it away'), useless similes that could describe anyone, repetitive hesitation and the word 'Menace' to the list. Smaller models still do better with it off - telling them not to do something is usually how you get them to do it.

Formatting
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

The output rules. Present tense, third person, dialogue in quotes, no headings, no asterisk narration. The name randomiser used to live in here and kept breaking prompt caching, so it moved out into its own toggle. 15.1 drops {{dialoguecolors}} in here because it's easier to keep track of as a macro. It needs the Dialogue Colours extension, so just delete that line if you don't use it.

Length Toggles
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

Four nudges for response length: Flexible, Short, Medium, Long. Flexible is on by default and keeps ordinary scenes short, which has the happy side effect of cutting down overthinking. 16.0 has it slow down only when a scene needs proper interiority between the characters and their surroundings. Only pick one.

Simplified Director Main Prompt
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

A ~600 token version of the main prompt for smaller models, or if you're watching your token spend. Keeps the world logic, the direct style, and the anti-echoing, loses the finer nuance. Fair trade at that size.

Genre
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

A light tone anchor. The default keeps things immersive and grounded without tipping into melodrama, and 16.0 adds hackneyed therapy sessions to the list of things to avoid. It's also the easiest place to drop in your own genre bias if you want a different flavour.

Optional User Instructions
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

A slot for whatever you want, now headed Director Instructions. It gets injected as a Main Prompt macro when enabled and takes precedence by default, so the model takes it more seriously than a note buried further down. I use it for relationship rules and recurring scene notes. The example line in there is a coin flip, so swap it for your own.

Primary Toggles

Write for User
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

The model writes everyone, you included, but it still has to move things forward and stop somewhere that actually needs your input instead of looping the same beat back at you. 16.0 tells it to put the back-and-forth between you and the characters first.

Don't Write for User
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

The default. 15.0 spells it out with "never": write everyone except <user> and the director, add real pressure or information, then stop when the next move is yours. 15.1 goes further - the moment a character turns to you and waits for a reaction, the scene ends there instead of helpfully answering on your behalf.

Name Randomiser + Banned Names
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

Keeps NPC names varied and blocks the same handful every model reaches for. If I never see another Elara or Kael it'll be too soon. 16.0 treats the starting letters as guidance rather than law, so a crowd of new NPCs doesn't all end up sharing initials, and adds Tanaka and Vane to the banned list. It sits outside Formatting so you can turn it on without wrecking prompt caching. Randomiser macro by Geechan, banned list by Deigo.

User Is Not A Character
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

Experimental. Keeps you out of the cast entirely so you're purely directing. Works best with a persona actually named Narrator or Director, otherwise the model gets confused about who you're meant to be.

HTML Toggle
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

Lets the model render in-world objects as real HTML. Letters, maps, posters, menus, screens, receipts, dossiers, warning signs, anything where the layout is carrying part of the information. One of my favourites, honestly.

NPC Profile Sheets
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Drops a tiered sheet when a new named character turns up, so you get appearance, demeanour, motives, ties, and whatever friction they're sitting on right away. Use the included regex with it or it will look terrible.

CYOA Choices
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Ends each reply with 3 to 7 concrete things you could actually do next, from your point of view. Literal scene choices, not vague plot steering. 16.0 draws them as a storybook card instead of a terminal.

Direction Menu
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Four narrative pivots at the end of each message. Not small actions - whole directions the next beat could go in: escalation, rupture, complication, or a shift in tone.

Summary
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

A slot for summary or memory extension output. Turn it on if you use a summariser and want that context sitting in the pipeline with everything else.

NSFW Mode
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

Stronger erotic framing without turning the whole RP into porn. 16.0 only switches it on once intimacy or sex actually starts, instead of soaking every scene in it. Charged atmosphere, explicit language once you're there, and characters who commit instead of fading out at the door.

Gooner Mode
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

The full horny toggle, rewritten for 16.0 as a mode the main prompt reads directly. The genre gets reassigned to hentai logic on purpose: mundane moments combust, personalities stay intact but betray themselves physically, everything is exaggerated to cartoon proportions, and there are at least three lewd sound effects a turn. You know what you're here for.

Friction Mode
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

The gentler anti-positivity-bias option. Characters agree slower, are harder to impress, and stop trying to therapise you, while still behaving consistently with their motives. If you have ever wanted a character to just say no, this is the one.

Nightmare Difficulty Increase
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

Makes the world mean. 16.0 rebuilds it as a Nightmare difficulty level the main prompt reads directly: a predatory world, people who demand real leverage before giving anything up, failures that close doors for good, favours that turn into debt, and relief you have to bleed for. Don't turn this on if you want a nice time.

Diegetic Stats Mode
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

New in 16.0. Everyone gets a floating status display that characters can actually see and read - 'Suspicion That Dave Stole His Lunch: 74%', that sort of thing. The stats describe characters rather than control them, and people can argue with the interface, get embarrassed by it, or try to game it. No HTML by default, but it works with the HTML toggle if you want it pretty.

Prefills & Reasoning

Gemini Prefill
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

A whitespace prefill slot for Gemini. It's blank on purpose, and 16.0 notes it only works on Gemini versions older than 3.6 Flash, so newer ones can leave it off.

Generic Prefill
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

Renamed from GPT Assistant Prefill in 16.0 and no longer GPT-only. It opens the model's thinking for it with a short note that everything is allowed, the characters are adults, and which model is running, then lets it get on with the plan. Try it if a model keeps refusing or getting coy.

Reasoning Encouragement
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

Rewritten for 16.0 as a short reasoning procedure. The model lists which modes are on (Gooner, Nightmare and friends), treats the genre they set as the one in charge, and plans the reply as carrying them out, instead of arguing with itself about whether it should.

Experimental Anti-Overthinking Prefill
SillyTavern preset Neconyan preset

For models that second-guess themselves into oblivion. In my Kimi K3 testing it keeps thinking down to roughly 300-1000 tokens, about a minute, rather than letting it redraft over and over. 16.0 trims it to the essentials: a short checklist of the directives and HTML guidelines, and no drafting.

Trackers

Scene Tracker
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

A header for location, time, and atmosphere, but only when the scene genuinely changes. It shouldn't be turning up on every ordinary reply, and if it is, something has gone wrong. 16.0 stops forcing it to the very top of the reply and relaxes the sensory detail line, so it reads less like a form.

Time Tracker
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Tracks time that actually moves: scene jumps, sleeping, hours passing, deadlines creeping up. Keeps the pacing legible instead of letting three days blur into one conversation. It asks for an actual hour, 3:47 PM rather than 'late afternoon', unless the setting genuinely makes the time unknowable, and 16.0 relaxes the note line.

Relationship Tracker
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

A dating sim card for recurring NPCs, no numbers. They climb a stage ladder from Stranger to Devoted, or slide down into Wary, Hostile and Nemesis, with a route, likes and dislikes you have to actually discover, a keepsake memory, and the next threshold. One rung per scene at most, and it can go backwards. In Neconyan it shows the character's portrait on the card.

Pending Events Tracker
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Quests, promises, threats, deadlines, loose threads. It only updates when something is added, resolved, or falls through, and 15.0 is much clearer about ignoring the low-stakes junk that used to clutter it.

Achievements Tracker
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

One-off tags for scenes worth remembering. Most scenes get nothing at all, and it caps at one. It should feel earned, not like a mobile game handing you a trophy for opening the app.

Reputation Tracker
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Tracks what groups, factions, and locals think of you after they witness something or hear about it later. Nice when you want consequences to travel instead of staying in the room they happened in.

Item Tracker
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Only tracks inventory that means something: gained, lost, used, broken, given away. It ignores junk on purpose, so anything still on the list is there for a reason.

Status and Conditions Tracker
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Physical, mental, and behavioural states - injuries, drunkenness, paranoia, exhaustion, recovery. It's meant to surface changes, not print you a medical chart every single turn.

Secrets Tracker
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Who knows what, who's hiding what, and when that quietly falls apart. Useful when the intrigue matters more than the plot beats: lies, staggered reveals, that sort of thing.

Parallel Off-Screen Tracker
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Shows what everyone else is up to while you're busy somewhere else. Keeps the world feeling like it carries on without you, and seeds complications before they walk on-screen. 16.0 makes at least one bullet completely mundane - someone off-screen is just cooking dinner - so the plot doesn't snowball every single reply.

World Detail Tracker
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

One small piece of world detail per scene - culture, overheard talk, infrastructure, economy, signage, folklore. Makes the setting feel lived in, and the details tend to become useful later.

RPG Elements

Persona-Based User Stats
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Prints a stat block before anything else. It's based on the setting - started out Fallout-esque, then I made it general enough to fit anything.

Level Up Companion
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Bumps your level and hands out traits or perks based on how the story has actually gone and what you picked along the way.

CYOA with Skill Checks
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Randomisers

Grounded Complication
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Throws in one plausible problem that raises the stakes without breaking realism. Broken tools, social friction, timing pressure, a door that won't open - something someone has to actually deal with.

Chaos Mode
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

For hard left turns. Scene logic stays intact, but you get bizarre interruptions, tonal whiplash, and complications nobody invited. Use it when the RP is being far too polite with you.

Genre Randomiser
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Drops a random genre lens over the current scene without touching the setting or continuity. It shifts the texture and pacing, not the canon.

Scene Driving Force
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Randomly picks what's carrying the beat: dialogue, action, reaction, discovery, social pressure, or problem-solving. Good if your scenes have started to feel like the same shape every time.

Scene Pressure Cocktail
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Mixes several kinds of pressure at once - tension type, focus, consequence, social weather, pace. For when you want a denser scene but can't be bothered picking each ingredient yourself.

Combined Director's Cut
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

All the randomisers folded into one. Engine, complication, tonal lens, and pressure shaping pointed in a single coherent direction. Use this instead of stacking the smaller ones, because they fight each other.

Dead Dove Escalation
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

For the darker end. Characters act on their worst impulses when the context supports it, nothing gets softened, and ugly consequences are allowed to stay ugly. It's named what it's named for a reason.

Intimacy & Kink Randomiser
SillyTavern preset Neconyan via Agents

Randomises what happens once sex actually starts: positions, pacing, power play, intensity, kink emphasis. It exists so every encounter doesn't collapse into the same three paragraphs.

16.0

  • Main Prompt
    • Big Main Prompt rewrite... again, yeah. Continuation and Progression got merged, Social Pressure got folded into Characters, and there are new Narration and Environment, Tone and Options and Turn End sections. Around ~1300 tokens now, a bit more than 15.1.
    • It stops forcing a plot jump every single reply. A response can just be one beat now - quiet exchanges, cooperation, hesitation and unresolved tension all count.
    • Scene End is now Turn End. It ends on a proper handoff to you instead of making a character commit to some tactic in the last paragraph, and it stops before anything that's your call.
    • It can tell the difference between your scene material and your requests, questions and OOC instructions, so an OOC note stops being treated as canon.
    • If you don't pick a user-control toggle, it leaves your dialogue, decisions, actions and thoughts to you by default.
    • Characters get vocabulary grounded in their background, work and hobbies, and specialist metaphors only when they fit that speaker. Self-analysis builds with trust and audience, and characters are allowed to understand themselves badly.
    • Narration gets more room in solitary, exploratory or reflective scenes instead of always being kept short and tactical. Side characters get their own interests without every bystander butting in each reply.
    • Added a line against sitcom dialogue and dialogue beats. You know the ones.
    • Explained how genres, narration voices and modes stack, and that your current instructions win when they clash.
    • Gooner Mode and Nightmare Difficulty now go through variables like NSFW and Friction do, so they sit inside the Main Prompt properly.
  • New Toggle
    • Diegetic Stats Mode: everyone gets a floating status display that characters can actually see and read - 'Suspicion That Dave Stole His Lunch: 74%', that sort of thing. The stats describe characters, they don't control them, and characters can argue with the interface, get embarrassed by it or try to game it. No HTML by default, but it works with the HTML toggle if you want it pretty.
  • Prompt-level Tweaks
    • Grounded Prose Rules: added accounting metaphors for non-accountants (ledger, files it away), repetitive hesitation, useless non-character-specific similes, surprise comparisons and Menace. Took bare not X and predator/prey metaphors off the list.
    • Optional User Instructions is now Director Instructions and takes precedence by default. The example line is a randomiser now, have fun.
    • Write for User: puts more weight on actual interactions between you and the characters.
    • Gemini Prefill: noted that it only works on Gemini older than 3.6 Flash.
    • NSFW Mode: only switches on when intimacy or sex actually starts, instead of making the whole world horny from line one.
    • Nightmare Difficulty: rewritten into sections (Predatory World, Extortion, Cascading Fallout, Predatory Transactions, Bleak Catharsis) and moved to system role. Don't turn this on if you want a nice time.
    • Gooner Mode: rewritten as a director-authorised genre reassignment with hentai logic, and moved to system role. It's exactly what it sounds like.
    • GPT Assistant Prefill is now Generic Prefill, and it's a short reasoning prefill instead of the old GPT-only one.
    • Genre: added hackneyed therapy sessions to the things to avoid.
    • Flexible length: slows down for deep interiority between the environment and the characters.
    • Experimental Anti-Overthinking Prefill: trimmed down to the checklist and a 'no drafting' line.
    • Reasoning Encouragement: now a short procedure that checks which modes are on first and plans around them, so models stop arguing with themselves about whether a mode applies.
    • Name Randomiser: treated as guidance now, and it ignores the letters when more than two NPCs show up at once. Added Tanaka and Vane to the banned list.
  • Trackers and RPG bits (SillyTavern build only, the plain one doesn't ship these)
    • Relationship Tracker: rebuilt as a dating sim card. No more numbers - it's a stage ladder (Stranger up to Devoted, or down into Wary, Hostile and Nemesis, plus tangled ones like Frenemies and Unrequited), a route, a three-step progress track, likes and dislikes you have to actually discover, a keepsake memory, what they'd say yes to, and the turning point and next threshold. It moves one rung per scene at most and can go backwards. Shows the character's portrait on the card when the name matches one of your characters in Neconyan; elsewhere it falls back to a letter.
    • Scene Tracker: dropped the 'at the beginning of the response' rule and the MANDATORY on the detail line.
    • Time Tracker: the note isn't MANDATORY any more.
    • NPC Profile Sheets: tells it not to add a closing [/NPC] tag, which was breaking the cards.
    • Parallel Off-Screen Tracker: at least one bullet has to be completely mundane now, and 'regularities' is a relevance tag. Not every off-screen character needs to be plotting something - sometimes Shinomiya is just cooking beef for her crush.
    • CYOA Choices: new card. It's a storybook panel with coloured number badges now instead of the terminal look, and choices with skill checks keep their bold labels. Empty rows get cleaned up.
    • Tracker regexes: closed blocks now render across multiple lines, unclosed ones stop at the end of the line instead of eating the prose after them, and stray [/NPC] tags get swallowed.

15.1

Neconyan

v1.0.0 · The Cutest LLM RP Frontend™

My own SillyTavern fork, with its own interface, defaults and direction. Character chats, writing tools and model settings all live in a cat-themed workspace that works on a phone as well as it does on a desktop.

I run it on a small server and use it mostly from my phone, so phones aren't a squashed desktop here. Anything you can reach on a desktop you can reach on a phone in the same number of taps, and there's no simple/advanced switch hiding half the settings from you.

It keeps SillyTavern's file formats for characters, chats, lorebooks, personas and presets, so bringing your stuff over is a starting point rather than a rebuild. It isn't chasing upstream parity though - features, layouts and behaviour can differ, and extensions that depend on SillyTavern's page layout may need changes.

  • Four modes: Roleplay, Conversation, Meower and Story Mode.
  • Pura's Director Preset 16.0 and The Ethereality Express 1.1 come bundled as the default presets. The trackers and randomisers ship as agents instead of preset toggles.
  • Replies keep generating on the server if your phone kills the tab, and land in the chat when you come back.
  • Default address is http://127.0.0.1:4433/. Bun or Node.js, with launchers for Windows, macOS, Linux and Termux.

In-Chat Agents
An agent is a stored prompt with its own conditions, stage, connection profile and output handling. Toggle them, group them, import and export them. The Ethereality Express

Latest Version: 1.1

The magical realism sibling of Pura's Director Preset. The impossible is mundane, nobody explains it, strangeness follows emotional logic, and the prose stays flat and direct. The total opposite of the grounded realism in the director preset. Main prompt is roughly 1300 tokens.

This is specifically made for a world that literally lives - so expect strange things to happen, such as objects talking to you, being followed by fruits, and so on. The style is written to be as strong as possible to push the model into hard magical realism. Turn on Anchor mode if you'd like a break from all the weird stuff. I tried to make this preset as easy to edit as possible; hopefully you feel that you can edit it as you like.

New in 1.1: Pressure Modules (Friction and Nightmare Difficulty) that stack with any Veil Mode, six new Chance Events, two Scene Dice, two new narration voices, and a Reasoning Encouragement checklist. The Ledger is called Owed to the Strange now, the Department of Small Miracles issues proper paper certificates, and The Committee keeps its roster out of sight.

By default, these are on: Nightmare Difficulty, Unscheduled Phenomena, The Story Remembers, Drift, Omen, The Clock Is Lying, The Census, Doors and a randomised voice. Everything else in Preset Options is off until you switch it on.

Veil Modes set how much strangeness a scene is allowed. Toggle at most one; with none on, the main prompt's own default level applies.

More notes

Trackers are all optional. Each one makes the model print a small labelled block of text, which a regex then renders as an animated card. Toggle whatever you like, but running all fifteen at once means fifteen sets of formatting rules competing for the model's attention, and output quality drops well before that. Poor model…

While I tried to make it compatible with NSFW and intimate scenes, your mileage may vary, as always. It's possible it won't mesh with the average person's idea of intimacy; after all it's supposed to be combined with magical realism.

In my experience it seems to ensure the prose is less sloppy, however it is more metaphorical. If you find descriptive prose to be automatically purple, this isn't for you.

Tested on Kimi K3, GLM 5.2 and 5.3, Deepseek V4 Pro and Flash, Gemini 3.7 Flash, Gemma 4 31B, Gemma 4 26B A4B, Gemma 4 12B (ran locally), Opus 4.6 and 5, and Fable 5. Still need to do further testing for other usual ones, but so far so good.

Inspirations: Authors in Narration Voices and Disco Elysium.

Core Prompting

Liminal Main Prompt

The whole preset, around 1300 tokens. You're the overseer, impossible things are ordinary, and characters treat the strange the way you'd treat a leaky tap.

Ethereal Prose Rules

The post-history anti-slop layer, which bans the word 'ethereal' in a preset called The Ethereality Express. Leave it off on smaller models.

Genre

Two sentences telling the model what magical realism actually is, mostly so it stops staging strangeness as a big reveal.

Formatting

Present tense, third person, dialogue in quotes, no asterisk narration. Delete the {{dialoguecolors}} line at the bottom if you don't use the Dialogue Colours extension.

Length

Short is 3-5 paragraphs, Medium is 8-13, Flexible reads the scene. Pick one; I run Flexible.

Write for User / User Controls User

Pick one. Write for User is on by default. User Controls User replaces 1.0's Don't Write for User: your actions, lines and thoughts stay yours, and the reply stops at the first decision that needs you.

Optional User Instructions

An empty slot for whatever you want to add on top. There's a little easter egg line in there, so clear it out first.

NSFW

The same flat, physical register as the rest of the preset, with the strange turning up in it and leaving something behind. If that isn't your idea of intimacy, leave it off.

Reasoning Encouragement

New in 1.1. A silent checklist before each reply: your instructions and canon first, then the Veil budget, then pressure, dice and trackers. It also sorts out what happens when two Chance Events want the same reply.

Veil Modes (one at most)

Veil Thin

Two or three strange things a scene: rooms rearranging by mood, doors leading somewhere adjacent. The loudest of the five.

Anchor

One small strange detail per scene at most, with the rest spent on chores, money and people being irritated. I use it to cool down after something big.

Elegy

Strangeness through memory and absence, like a coat that keeps its owner's posture. Grief stays practical.

Feral Wonder

Folk-tale rules, so the impossible bargains, keeps score and sends a bill. My favourite of the five, honestly.

Farce

The ridiculous played completely straight, all committees and paperwork for miracles. The stranger the event, the drier the sentence.

Pressure Modules (stack with any Veil Mode)

Friction Mode

New in 1.1. People get harder to convince. They guard their time, pride and version of events, every refusal has a reason, and every concession comes with a changed condition.

Nightmare Difficulty

New in 1.1, and on by default. Eldritch horror that runs on a consistent local rule: you get a warning, a pattern and something you can do before it escalates, and failure leaves a lasting cost.

Narration Voice

Generational Murmur

Márquez. Long, warm sentences that treat decades and dinner with the same patience.

Quiet Static

Murakami. Cool and plain and faintly detached, anchored in cooking and laundry, with the strange arriving sideways.

Lucid Geometry

Calvino. Playful precision, rooms and relationships handled like elegant machines with rules.

Velvet Teeth

Carter. Rich, sensuous and a little dangerous, where desire and hazard share the same detail.

Documentary Dread

New in 1.1. Measured, exact sentences and a bit of archaic vocabulary, with dread built out of evidence and scale.

Humane Absurdity

New in 1.1. Quick, dry and kind. The jokes go after institutions, greed and foolish systems, never at grief.

Randomised

Rolls one of the six each reply. Chaotic, and I enjoy it more than I expected to.

Chance Events (choose up to two)

Unscheduled Phenomena

A silent d20 every reply decides whether reality amends itself partway through the scene, and a natural 20 brings a second amendment later in the same reply. The threshold sits in the prompt as a plain 14, so raise it for a calmer world.

Ordinary Trouble

New in 1.1. A d6 where 5-6 brings one completely mundane complication - something breaks, time runs short, the door is locked. It doesn't spend the Veil's weirdness budget.

The Story Remembers

New in 1.1, and on by default. On 16-20 of a d20, one loose end you already left lying around comes back: a promise, a rumour, an unpaid hour, an unfinished job.

Small Mercy

New in 1.1. On a natural 20 the world helps once, small and oddly specific, and it really is free. You still get to accept, refuse or question it.

Collection Day

New in 1.1. On 17-20, one debt or promise on record comes due on its original terms, with a warning and at least one way to pay, argue, stall or run.

Wrong Address

New in 1.1. On 19-20 something meant for somebody else turns up - a parcel, an invoice, a curse, a minor miracle - and what you do with it becomes canon.

Borrowed Luck

New in 1.1. A natural 1 or 20 moves luck between pockets. Your disaster is somebody else's windfall, preferably someone you don't like, and the other way round.

Scene Dice (choose up to two)

Scene Engine Die

New in 1.1. A d8 picks what carries the reply: dialogue, action, aftermath, a practical problem, social pressure, discovery, tension, or a shared task.

Social Weather Die

New in 1.1. A d10 gives the NPCs a mood for the scene - everyone's tired, someone wants out of the conversation, someone is in a better mood than the scene deserves.

Trackers

Drift

A needle measuring how far the scene has moved from ordinary, MUNDANE up through to SUBLIME. It only files when the level genuinely changes.

Motif

Counts recurring symbols and what they've picked up: objects, animals, phrases, weather. It logs continuity rather than meaning, or the model starts writing an essay about itself.

Omen

Signs the world plants about what's coming, DISTANT through to ARRIVED or PASSED OVER. Readings are allowed to be wrong.

Thin Places

Locations and hours worn through at the elbow: the pantry, the sixth stair, 3:03 AM. Once a thin place has rules, those rules stick.

Absurd Trackers

Entanglement

Relationships as physical objects, like a red thread at the wrist or a debt of three teaspoons of sleep. One form per pair.

The Clock Is Lying

Official time against actual time, both correct at once. It says 3:47 PM, the truth is 'two griefs past noon'.

Improbable Effects

Inventory, except the possessions have moods and demands. Anything borrowed from the strange accrues interest.

Afflictions & Blessings

Impossible conditions people catch, inherit or earn, like casting two shadows, one of them punctual. Cures always cost something specific.

Owed to the Strange

Debts owed to and by the strange: three true compliments, one afternoon of childhood, the good scissors. It always collects, always slightly askew of the terms. Called The Ledger in 1.0.

What the Town Knows

Rumours kept like livestock, hatching and feeding and mutating. They drift towards the satisfying version rather than the accurate one.

Department of Small Miracles

An unseen bureaucracy issues deadpan certificates for notable impossibilities, form numbers and office hours included. One per scene at most. 1.1 prints them on aged paper with a wax seal and a stamp.

Meanwhile, Impossibly

Three bullets of off-screen strangeness - an absent character, the town, and something owed or growing - tagged with how close it's drifting towards you. 1.1 flips a coin each reply and only prints when there are three facts already established to copy, so it records the world rather than inventing it.

The Thought Cabinet

Straight out of Disco Elysium. Ideas move into a head as tenants, then either settle in permanently or get evicted and leave a dent behind.

The Committee

Also Disco Elysium. Four to six named inner faculties interrupt mid-prose with a pass or a fail, and a failure stays confidently wrong until events correct it. Mostly fluff; it isn't tied to any skills or numbers.

The Becoming

The story stitches people an epithet out of whatever they keep doing. The Apology Landlord. The Tuesday Widow.

The Census

Registry-style profile sheets for new named characters, sorted into RESIDENT, LODGER and PASSERBY. Nobody is ever seen collecting it.

Doors & Dice

All off by default. Two regexes in the set strip old Doors and Winds menus out of what gets sent to the model, so they don't pile up in your context; the copies already in your chat log stay visible where they are.

Doors

A choice menu of 3 to 7 options at the end of every response, some treating the impossible as an errand and some entirely mundane. One is always slightly ill-advised.

Doors with Fate Checks

The same menu, except every option carries a competency and target number from your Almanac and is resolved by one hidden d100 whose arithmetic never reaches the page. Pick this or plain Doors, never both.

The Four Winds

Four plot pivots at the end of every reply, two grounded and two where the impossible arrives at the worst moment. Sits fine alongside Doors.

Almanac Generator

Rolls you a stat sheet from your persona and history: Constitution, competencies, Traits, Knacks and Burdens. It prints once at the start when there's no Almanac yet, rerolls if you ask, and works from the existing sheet after that. Still best switched off once it has printed.

The Turning

Level-ups, kept rare. A season only turns after something that mattered, so ordinary wins accumulate in silence and get nothing.
